Runs the TE-0038 pre-registration (`tools/round_tier_strata.py`). 25,268 walk-forward
out-of-fold rows joined to exchange prices and corpus context, **zero excluded** — round
and tier are complete across the corpus. Metric is log-score gain over the exchange
de-vigged price, day-clustered bootstrap, 2,000 draws, seed 20260730.

## Verdict: NULL

The declared contrast's interval clears zero, and that is **not** sufficient, because the
declared statistic is *max minus min* — it picks the extremes and then tests them. Under
a null in which all three strata have identical means (each stratum centred on its own
mean, days resampled, sizes and within-day correlation preserved), a spread at least this
large occurs **19% of the time**. The result is what selecting the largest of three gaps
produces by chance.

**No context differential is established. Nothing is licensed** — no filter, no threshold,
no feature, no change to the served rule.

## Two errors caught, both worth recording

**1. The contrast estimator was not measuring a difference of means.** The first run
reported +0.002611 with CI [−0.000040, +0.001677] — an interval that does not contain its
own point estimate, which is impossible for a correct bootstrap. Cause: it pooled MIDDLE's
gains with EARLY's *negated* gains and averaged the pool; with 18,199 against 4,890 rows
that is a size-weighted blend, not a difference. Fixed to resample days and difference the
stratum means within each draw; verified against a synthetic pair with known difference
1.0 and deliberately unequal per-day sizes, recovering [0.9935, 1.0111].

**Had the impossible interval not been noticed, the bug's direction was conservative** —
it reported SPANS ZERO where the corrected estimator says CLEARS ZERO. The right answer
arrived anyway, but by luck, not by construction.

**2. The declared test was anti-conservative.** TE-0038 specified max-minus-min without
an adjustment for having selected the extremes. That is a flaw in the pre-registration
itself, not in the code. It is recorded here rather than quietly corrected, and future
pre-registrations naming an extremum must declare the selection-adjusted null alongside it.

## The hypothesis this does NOT establish

EARLY's point estimate is negative (−0.00047) while MIDDLE's is positive. A coherent
mechanism exists: early draws are full of qualifiers and low-history players, exactly
where the rating state is thinnest, so the model plausibly adds least — or subtracts —
there. Note what that story is about: **where our information is good, not where the
market is wrong.** If it were ever established, it would argue for *abstention* on
thin-data matches, not for a betting filter on middle rounds.

It is not established. It is a hypothesis generated by a null result, and it may only be
tested prospectively, pre-registered, on data this analysis has not seen.
